Words

Dial
A clock with N marks. The 360 dial has 360 marks, like the degrees in a circle.
Mark
One position on a dial.
Step
How many marks you jump each time.
Divides
Goes in exactly, with nothing left over. 6 divides 360. 7 does not.
Leftover (mod)
What is left after dividing. 361 leaves 1 after dividing by 360, so “361 mod 360” is 1.
Wrap-around
Counting on a dial, where the last mark is the same place as 0.
Prime
A number that only divides by 1 and itself: 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13.
Factor
A number that divides another one. The prime factors of 60 are 2, 2, 3 and 5.
Square grid, honeycomb grid
Two ways to lay points on a flat surface. The honeycomb is the “flower of life” pattern.
Wheel
A small dial that is one part of a bigger one. The thesis calls it a rod. Wheels of 8, 9 and 5 beads make 360.
Base
How many different digits you use before you carry. Base 10 has ten digits. Base 60 has sixty.
0;20
How base 60 is written. It means 20 sixtieths, which is one third.
Special prime
A prime p, 7 or more, where a dial of p − 1 marks fits into 144000 exactly. The project calls these qualifying primes.
Sign
Plus or minus. Which way a number points from the middle.
Mirror
Flips a point left to right, or top to bottom, around the middle.
Bit, byte
A bit is a 0 or a 1. A byte is 8 bits.
Kissing
Two circles or balls touching at exactly one point.
Packing
Fitting equal balls into space. The better the packing, the less empty space is left.
Dimension
2D is flat. 3D is space. 4D has one more direction. 4D can be worked out but not drawn.
Neighbour
A ball that touches the middle ball.
Proof
A step-by-step argument with no gaps.
Machine-checked
A proof that a computer program (Lean) has checked line by line.
Theorem
A statement that has been proved.
